The city council has approved a plan to replace all diesel buses with electric ones over the next six years. The first batch of 120 electric buses will start running on the busiest routes by March next year, while the remaining vehicles will be added in phases. Officials said the move is expected to cut annual fuel costs by nearly a third and reduce carbon emissions from public transport by more than 40 percent.

The project will cost about 900 crore rupees, of which almost half will come from a central government scheme for clean transport. The rest will be raised through a mix of state funds and a loan from a development bank. The council also announced that 15 new charging depots will be built on unused land owned by the transport department, and each depot will be able to charge up to 60 buses overnight.

Not everyone is happy with the plan. Several bus drivers' unions have raised concerns about job losses, since electric buses need fewer repairs and fewer mechanics. The council responded that every existing worker will be offered retraining, and that new roles in battery maintenance and charging operations will be created. Commuter groups have welcomed the plan but asked for a clear timeline, because earlier promises about new buses were delayed for years.

Experts say the biggest challenge will be the electricity supply. Charging hundreds of buses at the same time can put heavy pressure on the local grid, so the council is talking to the power distribution company about solar panels on the depots and battery storage to manage the load. A pilot run on two routes last year showed that the electric buses were quieter and cheaper to run, but their range dropped noticeably in very hot weather, something the manufacturers say will be fixed with a newer battery model.
